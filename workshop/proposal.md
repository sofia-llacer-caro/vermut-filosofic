# Submission title *
Friction First: Co-Building an Open Playbook for Critical Thinking Education in the AI Age

# Submission summary *
How do we teach critical thinking in classrooms flooded with AI? This workshop kicks off a collective, open pedagogical playbook built by educators worldwide. Drawing from the Friction First framework presented at FAB26, participants will co-design strategies for critical inquiry in AI-saturated contexts. Hands-on and participatory, the session gives educators concrete tools and co-authorship of a living resource that grows with every contribution.

# Detailed information *
This workshop is the founding moment of an international, open community of educators committed to critical thinking in the age of multimodal AI. It grows directly from the research paper "Friction First: A Toolkit for Teaching Critical Thinking in an AI-Saturated Classroom", presented at FAB26, which proposes a pedagogical framework built around critical inquiry, technological sovereignty, and pedagogical friction.

The session is structured in three parts: (1) a short introduction to the Friction First framework and its five design principles; (2) collaborative working groups where participants adapt the principles to their own educational contexts and document concrete strategies; (3) a collective synthesis where contributions are gathered into the first version of the open playbook.
Participants leave with: a shared vocabulary for critical AI pedagogy; practical strategies adapted to their own contexts; and co-authorship of a living, freely accessible resource that will continue to grow after the conference through an internationally connected educator network.

The playbook invites ongoing contributions, critiques, and expansions from educators in Fab Labs, universities, schools, and informal learning spaces. FAB26 is the natural starting point: the global maker and education community gathered there is exactly the community this resource is built for and with.

# Additional information
This workshop is co-organized with Jorge Muñoz Zanón (Generalitat de Catalunya), co-author of "Friction First" and a graduate of the Master in Design for Emergent Futures at Fab Lab Barcelona. His grounding in maker culture and the lead author's research in participatory AI make this a genuinely interdisciplinary collaboration born from shared conviction, not institutional design.
The playbook initiated here is intentionally low-barrier: no academic affiliation required, no fixed format imposed. It is built to hold what works, what fails, and what is entirely context-dependent (all equally valuable contributions).
The workshop methodology is portable and repeatable: the same co-design process can be run at any educational gathering, making each iteration an expansion of the network started at FAB26. The goal is not a finished document but a living, evolving practice that belongs to everyone who contributes to it.
